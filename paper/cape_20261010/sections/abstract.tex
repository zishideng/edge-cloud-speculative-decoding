\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding splits naturally between the edge and the cloud: a small draft model on the device proposes tokens and a large cloud model verifies them. Every verification, however, crosses the network, and real networks do not hold still. Drafting the next block while the cloud verifies the current one (lookahead) hides the round trip, but the right amount of drafting depends on the delay, the draft speed and the text, so a window tuned for one condition is wrong for the next: across our four model pairs and two delays, the fastest fixed window is two, four or eight tokens. We present \cape{}, an edge-only controller that decides in every round how many tokens to draft on the critical path and how many to prefetch in the shadow of verification. \cape{} models decoding as a two-state Markov chain over prefetch hits and misses, prices the overlap with the measured round-trip distribution and the device energy of every speculative token, and needs no offline calibration, no draft probabilities and no change to the cloud server. On a Jetson Orin Nano drafting over campus Wi-Fi for four draft/target pairs from the Llama, Qwen and Gemma families (\E{x:requests} measured requests, ten methods), \cape{} is the fastest method on every pair when the network delay varies over time: \E{x:dyn:S:sp:rng} faster than the best fixed window chosen in hindsight and \E{x:dyn:S:pearl:rng} faster than PEARL. It beats the hindsight-optimal fixed window in \E{x:wins} of 12 pair--delay settings, and its energy setting cuts device energy per token by \E{x:dyn:E:sv:rng} at the same speed.
\end{abstract}
