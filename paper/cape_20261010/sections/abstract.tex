\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding splits naturally between the edge and the cloud: a small draft model on the device proposes tokens and a large cloud model verifies them. Every verification, however, costs a network round trip. Drafting the next block while the cloud verifies the current one (lookahead) hides this latency, but it spends scarce device energy on tokens that are discarded whenever the verifier rejects the speculated prefix. Measurements on a Jetson Orin Nano that drafts over campus Wi-Fi for a Llama-3.1-8B verifier on an RTX 3080 server show that drafting occupies \E{sync:high:share}--\E{sync:low:share} of a synchronous round, that verification time barely grows with draft length (\E{verify:draft}\,ms per draft token against a mean of \E{verify:mean}\,ms), and that board energy is predicted by wall time and draft-busy time ($R^2=\E{energy:rsq}$). We present \cape{}, an edge-only controller that keeps fresh drafting off the critical path. \cape{} models decoding as a two-state Markov chain over prefetch hits and misses. In every round it selects the fresh draft window and the lookahead length that minimize an energy-weighted time per expected token, using online estimates of acceptance, round-trip time and draft cost. \cape{} keeps strict greedy verification and requires no change to the cloud server. On \E{full:prompts} prompts from GSM8K, HumanEval and MT-Bench (\E{full:requests:fmt} measured requests), \cape{}'s speed setting raises throughput by \E{full:capes:fixed4:pooled:abssp} \E{full:capes:fixed4:pooled:spci} over the fastest fixed-window configuration, at \E{full:capes:fixed4:pooled:abssv} more energy per token. It is also faster than the best fixed window chosen separately for each network delay, by \E{full:capes:fixed4:low:abssp} and \E{full:capes:fixed8:high:abssp}. Its energy setting matches the fixed window's speed with \E{full:capee:fixed4:pooled:abssv} \E{full:capee:fixed4:pooled:svci} lower energy per token. \cape{} is \E{full:capee:sync4:pooled:abssp}--\E{full:capes:sync4:pooled:abssp} faster than synchronous speculative decoding and \E{full:capee:adaedl:pooled:abssp}--\E{full:capes:adaedl:pooled:abssp} faster than AdaEDL.
\end{abstract}
