\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Edge--cloud speculative decoding pays a network round trip for every verification. Our measurements on a Jetson Orin Nano show that synchronous drafting occupies a large share of each round, that verification cost hardly depends on the draft length, and that draft work has a predictable energy price. \cape{} turns these observations into a two-state Markov model of the pipeline and chooses, in every round, how many tokens to draft on the critical path and how many to prefetch in the shadow of verification. It runs entirely on the device, keeps strict greedy verification, and exposes one weight that moves it along the speed--energy frontier. On \E{full:prompts} prompts, its speed setting is \E{full:capes:fixed4:pooled:abssp} faster than the fastest fixed window and faster than the best fixed window for each delay, while its energy setting saves \E{full:capee:fixed4:pooled:abssv} of the energy per token at equal speed. Both settings are far faster than synchronous decoding and AdaEDL. Evaluations on more devices, model pairs and real network traces, online calibration of the energy model and cloud-side incremental verification are the next steps.
