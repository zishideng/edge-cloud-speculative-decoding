\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Edge--cloud speculative decoding pays a network round trip for every verification, and that round trip changes from second to second. \cape{} answers one question in every round: how many tokens to draft on the critical path, and how many to prefetch in the shadow of verification. It answers it with a two-state Markov model of the pipeline that prices the measured round-trip distribution, the interruption of invalid lookahead and the device energy of every speculative token, entirely on the device and without calibration. On four model pairs from three families, \cape{} is \E{x:S:sync:rng} faster than synchronous speculative decoding in every setting, the fastest of nine methods on every pair whenever the delay varies, and never more than \E{x:S:regret} behind the fastest method of any setting. With one weight, it matches the speed of the best fixed window chosen in hindsight and uses up to \E{gemma3:dyn:E:best:svm} less energy per token. In edge--cloud speculative decoding, the question is not how many tokens to draft, but where to draft them.
