\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Edge--cloud speculative decoding pays a network round trip for every verification, and that round trip changes from second to second. \cape{} answers one question in every round: how many tokens to draft on the critical path, and how many to prefetch in the shadow of verification. It answers it with a two-state Markov model of the pipeline, the measured round-trip distribution and an explicit price on device energy, entirely on the device and without calibration. On four model pairs from three families, \cape{} is the fastest of ten methods whenever the delay varies, beats the hindsight-optimal fixed window in \E{x:wins} of 12 settings, and with one weight trades its speed lead for \E{x:dyn:E:sv:rng} lower energy per token. In edge--cloud speculative decoding, the question is not how many tokens to draft, but where to draft them.
