\section{\cape{} Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Overview}
At the start of each round, \cape{} chooses a fresh window $\gamma_f$ and a lookahead length $h$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cape}). If the previous lookahead is reusable, \cape{} sends all $h-1$ reusable tokens at once and drafts fresh tokens only if fewer than $\gamma_f$ remain, so no drafting delays the request. Otherwise it drafts $\gamma_f$ fresh tokens on the critical path, usually only one or two. As soon as the request is sent, \cape{} drafts $h$ lookahead tokens in the background while the cloud verifies. Short fresh windows keep rounds short after misses, and long lookahead converts verification time into ready-to-send drafts after hits (Fig.~\ref{fig:timeline}(c)).

\subsection{Two-state pipeline model}
Let $c$ and $d$ be the per-call and per-token draft costs, $V$ the verification round trip from sending a draft to receiving the verdict, and $\alpha_M$ and $\alpha_H$ the per-token acceptance probabilities in miss and hit rounds. For a candidate $(\gamma_f,h)$, background drafting takes $b=c+dh$. A round ends only when both verification and lookahead have finished, which takes $o=\max(V,b)$ after the request is sent.

\emph{Miss rounds} draft $\gamma_f$ tokens first:
\begin{align}
T_M &= c+d\gamma_f+o, & B_M &= c+d\gamma_f+b,\nonumber\\
N_M &= \textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{\gamma_f}\alpha_M^{\,i}, & p_{MH} &= \alpha_M^{\,\gamma_f+1}.\label{eq:miss}
\end{align}
Here $N_M$ is the expected number of emitted tokens (accepted tokens plus the target token) under geometric acceptance, and $p_{MH}$ is the probability that all $\gamma_f$ tokens and the bonus guess are accepted, which makes the next round a hit.

\emph{Hit rounds} send $s=\max(h-1,\gamma_f)$ tokens, of which $u=s-(h-1)$ must be drafted as a top-up:
\begin{align}
T_H &= [u>0](c+du)+o, & B_H &= [u>0](c+du)+b,\nonumber\\
N_H &= \textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{s}\alpha_H^{\,i}, & p_{HH} &= \alpha_H^{\,s+1}.\label{eq:hit}
\end{align}
The stationary hit share of the chain is $\pi_H=p_{MH}/(p_{MH}+1-p_{HH})$. We write $\bar X=(1-\pi_H)X_M+\pi_H X_H$ for $X\in\{T,B,N\}$.

\emph{Objective.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy}, board energy is proportional to $T+rB$, where $r$ is the ratio of draft-active to base power. \cape{} minimizes
\begin{equation}
J(\gamma_f,h)=\frac{\bar T+w\,(\bar T+r\bar B)}{\bar N},\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
which is the time per token plus $w$ times the energy per token, expressed in seconds of base power. With $w=0$, \cape{} optimizes speed only. Larger $w$ makes speculative work more expensive and shortens the lookahead when acceptance is low.

The model captures the trade-offs of Section~\ref{sec:measure}. A larger $\gamma_f$ yields more tokens per miss round but lengthens the critical path. A larger $h$ costs no time while $b\le V$ and makes a hit more valuable, but it raises $B$ in every round, hit or miss. Separate acceptance rates for the two states capture the correlation of acceptance across rounds: hits happen in predictable stretches of text, where the next block is also more likely to be accepted. In our main evaluation, late-request estimates of $\alpha_H$ average \E{ctl:full:capes:low:ahit}, against \E{ctl:full:capes:low:amiss} for $\alpha_M$.

\subsection{Online estimation}
\cape{} re-estimates its inputs before every round and resets them for every request. $\hat\alpha_H$ is estimated from rounds that follow a fully accepted round, the precondition of a hit, and $\hat\alpha_M$ from all other rounds. Each estimate uses the last 32 such rounds and a prior of 8 accepted tokens out of 10 examined:
\begin{equation}
\hat\alpha_s=\min\Big(0.99,\ \frac{8+\sum_j n_j}{10+\sum_j \big(n_j+[n_j<\gamma_j]\big)}\Big),
\end{equation}
where round $j$ of state $s$ sent $\gamma_j$ tokens and $n_j$ of them were accepted. $\hat V$ is the median of the last three verification round trips (150\,ms before the first one completes). It follows a change in delay within a few rounds while ignoring single spikes. The per-token draft cost $\hat d$ is the median, over recent background calls, of the call duration minus the overhead $c=10$\,ms, divided by the number of tokens drafted (24.5\,ms before the first call). The power ratio is fixed at $r=2.3$, calibrated on development runs.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One \cape{} round on the device}
\label{alg:cape}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require prefix $x$; pending lookahead $L$ drafted from $x'$; estimates $\hat\alpha_M,\hat\alpha_H,\hat V,\hat d$
\State $(\gamma_f,h)\gets\arg\min J(\gamma_f,h)$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}}
\If{$x=x'\Vert\ell_1$} \Comment{hit}
  \State $D\gets\ell_{2:h}$, topped up to $\gamma_f$ tokens
\Else \Comment{miss}
  \State draft $\gamma_f$ tokens $D$ from $x$ \Comment{critical path}
\EndIf
\State send $(x,D)$ to the verifier
\State draft $h$ tokens $L$ from $x\Vert D$ in the background
\State receive $(n,t)$; $x\gets x\Vert d_{1:n}\Vert t$
\State update $\hat\alpha_M$ or $\hat\alpha_H$, $\hat V$ and $\hat d$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Decision and execution}
\cape{} evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} for all $\gamma_f\in\{1,\dots,\Gamma\}$ and $h\in\{2,\dots,\Gamma+1\}$ with $\Gamma=8$. These 64 candidates take about \E{overhead:ms}\,ms in Python on the Jetson CPU, less than 0.2\% of a round. Lookahead is capped by the remaining token budget and skipped in the last round. Lookahead still pending at the end of a request is awaited and charged to its wall time and energy.

\emph{Exactness.} \cape{} changes only which draft tokens are proposed, never how they are verified. A prefetched block is reused only if the full verified prefix, including the bonus token, equals the prefix from which it was drafted. Every emitted token is therefore the target model's top choice for its prefix. In exact arithmetic, \cape{} would produce the same output as any other strict greedy configuration. With a quantized target served in floating point, near-tied scores can resolve differently when the verification computation has a different shape; Section~\ref{sec:integrity} quantifies this effect.
